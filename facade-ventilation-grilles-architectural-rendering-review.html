How to review facade ventilation grilles in architectural renderings

A practical review method for making intake and exhaust openings visible, credible and traceable to current design information.

Why ventilation grilles change a facade image

A residential facade can appear carefully composed while the ventilation openings are absent, undersized or moved to an implausibly tidy location. Air intakes, exhaust grilles and transfer openings may interrupt cladding modules, reveal dark recesses and influence how a building reads from the street. The purpose of this review is to represent an agreed design faithfully. It is not a ventilation calculation, fire-safety assessment or substitute for mechanical design.

Identify the correct source drawings

Request the latest architectural elevations, facade setting-out drawings, building-services plans, equipment or grille schedule and relevant consultant comments. Establish which openings are confirmed and which remain provisional. Note whether each opening is an intake, exhaust, pressure relief or another system element, without guessing its function from its shape. A dated source register helps avoid carrying obsolete openings into the final render.

Check openings against rooms and plant routes

Compare each opening with the corresponding floor level, room or riser and facade zone. A grille that appears aligned in elevation can still conflict with a balcony, canopy, window or neighbouring facade return. Do not relocate it merely to improve symmetry. If the services drawing and architectural elevation disagree, request a coordinated decision and record which version the image follows.

Preserve the visible opening size

Use approved dimensions for the clear opening, outer frame and any surrounding reveal. A texture applied to a wall is not equivalent to a recessed opening: it can erase depth, shadow and the construction joint. At eye level, even a modest change in width can affect the apparent rhythm of brickwork or panels. If a precise product is unknown, show a labelled indicative envelope instead of a falsely detailed proprietary grille.

Model blades, mesh and depth with restraint

Blade pitch, orientation, frame thickness and the darkness behind the grille determine how it reads in CGI. A fully black rectangle can exaggerate its visual weight; a perfectly reflective or transparent panel can make it disappear. Base the geometry on the specified type where available. Avoid invented insect mesh, visible fans or internal ducts unless they are documented and actually visible from the selected camera.

Coordinate cladding modules and finishes

Check whether the grille sits flush, proud or recessed relative to brick, timber, render or rainscreen panels. Review cut edges, joint alignment, trims and likely colour contrast against the approved facade palette. A vent should not float across brick bonds or interrupt a facade expansion joint without a coordinated detail. Use the same material references and colour-management workflow as the rest of the facade.

Do not turn a visual image into an airflow claim

A rendering cannot demonstrate air volume, pressure drop, discharge separation or compliance with ventilation guidance. Screening, dense planting and decorative louvres may look attractive but can introduce technical constraints. The mechanical designer must confirm any free-area requirement, clearance or relationship between intake and exhaust. Show only the approved geometry, and do not add arrows or performance labels unless supplied for a separate technical diagram.

Treat water marks and weathering as evidence-led

Exhaust openings may be associated with local staining or condensation in some conditions, but that is not inevitable and should not be fabricated as a prediction. Conversely, a perfectly clean CGI surface must not be used to imply maintenance-free operation. For a new-build marketing image, depict the proposed finish; for a verified existing-building photomontage, preserve documented weathering and adjacent facade conditions.

Review balconies, planting and pedestrian viewpoints

Check views from the street, courtyard, nearby balconies and the principal marketing camera. A grille hidden by a conveniently placed shrub may be clearly visible in winter or from a different angle. Avoid using foliage to conceal unresolved equipment. Where an opening is close to a pedestrian route or private outdoor space, note the visual relationship and refer technical concerns to the relevant consultant.

Test daylight and night views consistently

Recesses and blades can become much darker in low-angle daylight or evening imagery. Check whether the render uses a plausible light direction and whether the same grille appears consistent across the approved views. Do not invent glowing interiors, fan silhouettes or operational lighting. If a night scene is requested, distinguish visible architectural lighting from artistic fill and preserve the grille's actual location.

Fictional example: a courtyard apartment block

Fictional illustrative scenario, not a client commission: A proposed courtyard block has a brick ground floor and upper rendered facade. An early image omits two service openings because the mechanical layout was issued after the first model. The architect supplies a revised elevation identifying their approximate positions, while the engineer has not selected the final grille type. The visualisation team inserts correctly sized neutral placeholders, flags the product and blade direction as provisional, and issues a second courtyard camera for review. No airflow or acoustic performance is claimed.

Use a small decision log for each visible opening

For every opening, record its ID, facade and level, source drawing and revision, dimensions, finish, approval owner and visual status. Mark items as confirmed, provisional or excluded from the view. Attach annotated crops for comments rather than vague requests to make the facade cleaner. When a services revision moves an opening, update all affected camera views, not just the hero image.

Pre-release checklist for the architect and developer

Before approval, confirm that all scheduled visible openings are present; their positions match coordinated elevations; sizes and reveals are plausible; frames and louvres use documented geometry; facade joints and finishes remain consistent; planting does not disguise unresolved conflicts; lighting is credible; and the assumption register names each provisional element. A photorealistic result is a communication tool, not evidence that ventilation or fire requirements have been met.
