How to review automatic sliding entrance doors in architectural renderings

A visual QA guide for sliding entrance door openings, tracks, sensors, glazing and operating states, without confusing CGI with safety approval.

A convincing entrance is not an operational test

Automatic sliding doors can look deceptively simple in a hero render: two glass leaves and a thin header. But the apparent opening width, fixed side panels, operator housing and relationship to the approach all affect whether the image represents the design. This guide addresses visual correspondence with issued information. It does not assess door safety, accessibility compliance, fire performance or the suitability of an automatic door system.

Start with the door schedule, not a generic library model

Request the current entrance elevation, door schedule, reflected ceiling or soffit information, threshold section and selected manufacturer's approved arrangement where available. Record the revision and identify whether the operator is specified or provisional. A generic storefront component should not silently become a claim that a particular product has been approved. Confirm who owns decisions about door operation and life-safety interfaces.

Separate clear opening from total glazed frontage

The visible width of the entrance assembly is not the clear passage width. Fixed sidelights, sliding leaves, overlap and parking zones can consume space. Compare the issued opening configuration with both the closed and open model states. A central pair of sliding leaves should not magically disappear behind fixed glazing if the design does not provide a credible leaf travel path. Use an orthographic elevation before judging the perspective image.

Show where the leaves actually travel

A pocketing arrangement, surface-sliding arrangement and telescopic system have different visual implications. Check where each leaf rests when open, what remains visible through the glazing and whether a structural mullion blocks the implied path. If the issued documents do not define the configuration, label the render assumption as unresolved rather than choosing a more elegant system without approval.

Coordinate the operator header and surrounding soffit

Automatic door equipment often requires a header or concealed space, but its dimensions and service access depend on the selected system. The CGI should show the issued operator enclosure, transom and soffit geometry rather than flattening the entrance into uninterrupted glass. Check that the header aligns with adjacent facade modules and does not collide with canopy structure, lighting or signage. Never invent a minimum clearance or maintenance dimension.

Inspect the floor and threshold junction

At an entrance, small level differences matter visually. Compare the interior finish, exterior paving, documented threshold and drainage arrangement in section. Do not remove a raised edge with Photoshop merely to make the route look seamless. Equally, do not add a decorative channel that the architect has not specified. The accessible-route visual review covers the wider approach; the sliding-door check focuses on the assembly itself.

Treat sensors and activation devices as specified equipment

Presence detectors, activation buttons, card readers and emergency devices may be visible, concealed or integrated into the door system. Their location should come from approved coordination information, not from a random stock render. Check that an illustrated sensor does not imply verified detection coverage. For adjacent access devices, use the intercom and access-control review. Equipment shown in CGI is not proof of safe operation.

Check the glazing and manifestation in both states

Reflective glass can obscure the distinction between moving leaves and fixed panels. Use a neutral material pass to check leaf edges, overlaps and vertical mullions before adding strong reflections. Where manifestation or contrast markings are specified, preserve their placement and legibility; do not add arbitrary patterns to suggest compliance. Compare with the glass manifestation guide when the entrance forms part of a public interior.

Use two views with different jobs

A marketing camera can establish the entrance atmosphere but often hides the operating arrangement behind reflections, people or a strong diagonal perspective. Add a straightforward elevation crop or near-frontal coordination view. Inspect the door once closed and once open, with the same fixed context. A clear comparison reveals travel-path conflicts that a single attractive still can conceal.

Keep people and furniture out of the evidence

A person walking through a door suggests movement, not a verified activation sequence. Avoid using people, plants or display furniture to cover unresolved tracks, sensor positions or threshold geometry. If a door is shown mid-opening, identify that frame as an illustrative operating state, not an animation of a validated mechanism. Do not imply evacuation suitability or capacity from the number of figures in the image.

Fictional illustrative example: lobby redesign

Fictional illustrative example, not an Atmosferi client project or approved door detail. A proposed apartment lobby is shown with two frameless leaves disappearing behind a glazed side panel. During review, the architect notices that the current elevation specifies fixed sidelights and a different leaf arrangement. The artist replaces the generic asset with geometry aligned to the issued elevation, exports closed and open comparison crops, and marks the operator header as awaiting the supplier's detail. The exercise improves traceability, not system performance.

Record decisions before publishing the hero image

Use a compact register with entrance ID, drawing revision, selected or provisional system, closed-state crop, open-state crop, threshold reference, operator/header status, sensor/device status and the responsible reviewer. Keep unresolved items visible. Before final approval, compare the retouched hero image against the neutral geometry pass so that a polished reflection has not erased a door edge or suggested an impossible leaf path. Ask the design team to approve visual fidelity, not operational certification.
