How to review timber cladding board layout in architectural renderings

A practical review guide for timber cladding board direction, widths, joints, corners and openings in architectural renderings.

Board layout is architectural information

Timber cladding is often reduced to a convincing wood texture. But a façade's rhythm depends on the specified board orientation, nominal and visible widths, gaps, profiles, battens and termination details. A beautiful render with an incorrect board module can misrepresent the architecture just as clearly as a misplaced window. This review method is for architects and visualisation teams checking the image against design information. It is not a specification for weatherproofing, fire safety, fixing design or durability.

Establish a traceable source of truth

Start with the current elevation, wall build-up, cladding setting-out drawings, product profile where selected, and detail references at openings, corners and the base. Record revision identifiers and the status of each decision: specified, assumed for concept only, or unresolved. A timber photograph is useful for colour and grain reference but cannot tell the artist where board joints should fall. When there is no approved setting-out, request a design decision rather than inventing a neat repeat pattern.

Confirm vertical, horizontal and mixed orientations

Check each elevation separately. Vertical boards can emphasise height, while horizontal boards create long datum lines; alternating them casually changes the design. On mixed-material façades, note where orientation changes, where a screen differs from a weathering layer, and whether an overhang or soffit uses a separate layout. A rotated texture map is not necessarily a rotated board assembly: grooves, edge shadows and board ends must follow the intended direction.

Check visible widths, gaps and cumulative drift

Use documented dimensions to compare the number of boards across a known bay. Distinguish nominal board width from visible face width after overlap or profiling. A small scale error repeated across a whole elevation can create an extra board at the corner or an implausibly narrow strip beside a window. Compare the orthographic model or elevation against the render; do not estimate exact widths from perspective pixels. Treat the final partial board as a coordination question, not an invitation to stretch the texture.

Separate board edges from surface grain

Wood grain, knots and tonal variation should not be mistaken for board boundaries. Check that edges have consistent physical depth and gap treatment where the selected system calls for it. Avoid identical grain repeating every few boards, but also avoid randomising lengths or joints where a deliberate pattern is documented. For horizontal cladding, distinguish actual board joints from end joints and any staggered layout. Never let a procedural material generate a fictitious construction pattern.

Review corners and returns in three dimensions

Inspect an outside corner from two views. Does the cladding meet at a trim, a mitre, an expressed end grain or another documented detail? At internal corners, check whether the last board is cut, returned or concealed. Verify that the board rhythm continues or intentionally stops at recessed balconies and deep reveals. The goal is to represent the selected architectural detail, not to recommend one. An unresolved corner should remain flagged in the review record.

Inspect window heads, sills and reveals

Window openings reveal layout errors quickly. Compare the board grid with jamb locations, head and sill lines, ventilation zones and any trims shown on the drawings. A rendered board that seems to disappear behind a frame may conceal an unresolved closure. Check that openings are not subtly moved to make a repeating texture fit. Where a fire barrier or cavity detail is not visible, do not imply that the rendering proves its presence or compliance.

Check profiles, relief and lighting

A flat colour map can look acceptable in overcast light yet fail under grazing sunlight. Compare a neutral-light crop with the final atmospheric view to see whether the intended board profile and joint depth are represented. Excessively deep grooves may make slim timber boards resemble large panels; excessive bump mapping can exaggerate roughness. Review the appearance at the final output resolution, because narrow gaps may alias or vanish after downsampling.

Keep species, grain and finish distinct from geometry

A material sample may establish a finish direction without resolving every board's grain. Keep the approved tone, sheen and broad grain character separate from the geometric setting-out. If the timber is expected to weather, label the scenario and do not present a speculative aged appearance as a guaranteed outcome. For colour and surface approval, use the material-finish checklist; this guide specifically addresses board layout and visible construction rhythm.

Use two complementary review views

First compare a flat elevation or orthographic export against the setting-out drawing. Mark board counts, orientation changes and alignment at openings. Then review the chosen eye-level marketing camera for depth, visibility of gaps and how shadows alter the reading. A third close crop at a corner or jamb is often more informative than another distant hero image. Keep camera IDs and source drawing revisions with the comments so the correction can be verified rather than merely described.

Fictional example: a timber-clad housing entrance

Fictional illustrative scenario, not an Atmosferi client project or an approved construction detail. A proposed housing entrance uses vertical timber boards. The hero render looks coherent, but the material repeats at a different width on either side of the entrance and a corner has an implausibly narrow final strip. The architect supplies the current elevation and confirms that the corner termination remains provisional. The visualisation artist corrects the visible module, retains the provisional corner in the issue log, and shares an orthographic comparison alongside the revised marketing view.

Record ownership of unresolved details

For each discrepancy, log the façade zone, drawing revision, image or camera ID, expected condition, actual rendering condition and decision owner. Separate an artist-side mapping error from an architect-side design question. A review can approve fidelity to the issued design while explicitly leaving fixing, moisture management, fire classification, tolerances and construction sequencing to the appropriate specialists. Do not replace that distinction with a vague 'approved' label.

Final pre-publication checks

Confirm the board orientation on every elevation, visible module and board count, treatment of gaps and end joints, corners and reveals, window interfaces, profile depth, texture repetition, and consistency across camera views. Make sure provisional details are recorded and the rendering is tied to a specific drawing revision. For other façade linework decisions, consult the movement-joint review and the brick coursing guide; neither replaces the timber-specific setting-out check.

Review the timber layout before final export
