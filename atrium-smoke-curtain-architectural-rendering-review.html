How to review atrium smoke curtains in architectural renderings

A drawing-led checklist for representing atrium smoke curtains, headboxes, drop zones, lighting and open-space interfaces in architectural CGI.

Why smoke curtains deserve their own image review

A retractable smoke curtain can be nearly invisible in a polished atrium rendering, even though its headbox, side guides and deployment zone may affect the architecture. The problem is not whether a rendering proves a fire strategy. It cannot. The problem is whether a published image conceals a known architectural component or shows a configuration inconsistent with the coordinated design. This guide addresses visual fidelity only. Fire engineering, smoke control design, product specification and approval remain with the responsible specialists.

Collect the right drawings before modelling

Request the current architectural sections and elevations, reflected ceiling plans, fire and smoke-control strategy drawings where available for visual coordination, manufacturer or specialist details if selected, and lighting, signage and structural coordination information. Record drawing IDs and revisions. Establish whether the curtain is confirmed, a design allowance or merely one option under review. Do not invent a concealed cassette or a deployment route because a reference photograph looks cleaner.

Check the headbox against the ceiling design

Compare the curtain headbox or recess position with the ceiling datum, bulkheads, beams, acoustic linings and lighting tracks. A thin line in a perspective may represent a substantial coordinated zone in section. Where a ceiling slot is specified, depict its actual visible width and finish rather than deleting it in post-production. If the detail remains unresolved, label the image illustrative and ask the architect to confirm the intended visible treatment.

Preserve the deployment zone in the composition

A retractable curtain needs a documented intended path between its stored and deployed positions. In a rendering, check that the indicated line is not casually moved behind a column, across an uncoordinated stair landing or through a suspended feature. Avoid placing permanent furniture, hanging artworks, planters or signage across the drawn deployment zone without raising the conflict. The image review identifies potential visual inconsistencies; it does not calculate clearance, smoke performance or permitted obstructions.

Show side guides and edge conditions only where documented

Some arrangements have visible side guides or specific interfaces at walls, glazing and structural elements; others differ. Match the selected detail rather than assuming one universal system. Check whether columns, balustrades, glazed screens and wall finishes continue uninterrupted in the source drawings. A curtain line drawn straight through a glass balustrade may indicate missing coordination, not a licence for the visualiser to redesign the balustrade.

Coordinate lighting, sprinklers and ceiling services

Review the curtain position against pendant luminaires, linear lights, access panels, grilles, detectors and other visible ceiling elements. If drawings conflict, keep a discrepancy note with the responsible discipline and drawing revision. Do not silently move fire or building-services devices to improve symmetry. For broader ceiling representation, see the smoke detector visual review and sprinkler head coordination guide.

Separate everyday and deployed states

Most marketing images depict an atrium in its everyday configuration. If a second diagram or rendering shows the curtain deployed, clearly label it as an illustrative state and use the coordinated geometry. Do not imply that an attractive still frame verifies deployment speed, control logic, fire resistance or smoke containment. Where the client needs to explain the strategy to stakeholders, pair a neutral annotated section with the architectural view and obtain specialist review of technical labels.

Choose viewpoints that expose conflicts

Keep the hero camera for spatial experience, then prepare at least one ceiling-oriented view showing the headbox zone and one section or orthographic comparison of the curtain line against the atrium edge. Match model revisions across views. Wide-angle photography-like perspectives can hide shallow ceiling recesses and exaggerate clearances. A marked-up close view often resolves coordination questions faster than several additional beauty renders.

Fictional example: a hotel atrium renovation

Fictional illustrative scenario, not an Atmosferi commission or completed project. An imagined hotel atrium has a continuous timber-slat ceiling and a suspended lighting feature. An early image omits a smoke curtain because its position is not visible in the interior concept model. Later coordination drawings show a headbox near the atrium edge and a deployment line intersecting the light feature. The architect asks the fire specialist and lighting designer to resolve the interface. Only the agreed architectural geometry is then reflected in the final image. No approval, performance or safety result is claimed.

Maintain a concise discrepancy register

For each visible issue, note the location, source drawing and revision, whether the depiction is confirmed or illustrative, the responsible decision-maker and the affected camera IDs. Distinguish an artist's modelling correction from a design question requiring professional coordination. If the selected curtain detail changes, update every affected still, animation frame and marketing diagram. Keep earlier exports traceable so outdated imagery is not accidentally reused.

Pre-publication checklist

  • Smoke curtain status and design stage are identified.
  • Headbox, recess and ceiling datum follow current coordinated sections.
  • Deployment line is not hidden or repositioned for aesthetics.
  • Walls, glazing, columns and balustrades agree with documented interfaces.
  • Lighting and visible ceiling services are checked against the same revision.
  • Everyday and deployed states are labelled accurately when both are shown.
  • Neutral coordination views complement the hero perspective.
  • Unresolved elements have a decision owner and are not presented as approved.
  • All affected exports are updated after revisions.
  • Visual sign-off is not described as fire-safety or product compliance certification.

Use the rendering to expose questions early, not to replace fire engineering or authority review.
