How to review brick weep holes in architectural facade renderings.

A convincing brick facade can accidentally hide drainage-related openings or invent a repeating pattern. This source-led checklist keeps the image aligned with the issued details.

Why weep openings need a separate visual review

Brick weep openings are small, but they can be visible at the base of a cavity wall, above openings or near other documented drainage locations. An artist who paints an uninterrupted mortar joint can erase them; one who repeats dark slots across every course can invent a drainage strategy. This guide is about representing issued architectural information in images, not designing cavity drainage.

Ask for details, not a guessed spacing rule

Collect the current masonry elevations, cavity-wall sections, lintel and sill details, wall-base detail, relevant facade schedules and any selected vent or weep product reference. Record drawing numbers and revision dates. Ask the architect or facade consultant to identify which locations are fixed, provisional or outside the visualisation scope. A generic spacing pattern from an online photo is not a substitute for a project detail.

Distinguish weep openings from ordinary mortar joints

A weep may be a deliberate open perpend joint, a proprietary insert or another specified visible component. Its appearance differs from a normal mortar joint, movement joint, brick vent and damaged pointing. Verify the component type and visible face. Do not enlarge the void into a continuous horizontal gap or confuse it with the facade ventilation grilles discussed in our separate review.

Map positions to actual wall interfaces

Use the drawings to check where drainage openings are intended at wall bases, cavity trays, lintels, parapet interfaces and other documented conditions. Not every brick facade has the same build-up. Trace the positions around entrances, window heads, recesses and changes in ground level. If the source only shows a typical detail, do not automatically propagate it across the entire building without confirmation.

Coordinate the brick module and openings

Check the weep positions against brick bond, course heights, vertical perpend joints and special brickwork. A texture-only facade can place a slot halfway through a brick instead of in a joint. Review a corner and a typical bay at the same model scale. The brick coursing guide covers the wider masonry module; this review focuses specifically on drainage-related openings and their representation.

Choose geometry or texture according to camera distance

For close views, model the approved insert or opening with plausible recess depth and material finish. For distant marketing views, the same detail may occupy fewer than a few pixels and should not be artificially amplified. Avoid adding black dots in post-production without checking their locations. Use a controlled detail crop to establish that the geometry exists even when it is subtle in the hero perspective.

Check paving, landscaping and visible obstructions

A correctly located opening may appear visually blocked by a raised planter, paving edge or later landscape revision. Flag that apparent conflict to the architect rather than moving the opening yourself. The render can show the documented relationship between wall and finished ground level, but it cannot establish drainage function, moisture resistance or regulatory compliance.

Review daylight, shade and material contrast

Dark inserts can read like damage against light mortar; pale inserts can disappear in bright sunlight. Compare one neutral daylight crop with the final hero lighting. Keep brick, mortar and insert colours consistent with approved selections. If the manufacturer or finish has not been chosen, mark the representation provisional rather than inventing a branded product.

Fictional example: a courtyard apartment block

Fictional illustrative scenario, not an Atmosferi commission or an approved construction detail. A proposed courtyard elevation has brick cladding and a recessed entrance. The first render uses a seamless brick texture and shows no weep openings. The architect provides a revised wall-base and lintel detail indicating visible inserts at selected interfaces. The visualiser places those elements against the course module, marks one entrance condition unresolved and exports a neutral close crop plus the original camera. The image is not presented as evidence of moisture-management performance.

Record unresolved decisions and publication changes

Maintain a short register with facade zone, opening type, drawing reference, selection status, reviewer, affected camera IDs and image revision. Separate a missing render element from an unresolved design decision. If the wall detail changes after publication, check all affected images and brochure crops; the rendering change-control guide explains how to track those replacements.

Pre-publication visual QA checklist

  • Current elevations and cavity details are identified by revision.
  • Every depicted opening has a documented location or is marked provisional.
  • Weeps are not confused with movement joints or ordinary mortar joints.
  • Visible components align with the brick course and bond.
  • Wall-base, lintel and threshold conditions are checked where relevant.
  • Ground levels and landscape elements do not conceal an apparent conflict.
  • Close crops and wider cameras are consistent without exaggerated contrast.
  • No technical drainage, waterproofing or code-compliance claim is made.
  • Open questions and reviewer approvals are recorded.

Keep the brickwork image traceable to the design.

Send the latest masonry details, elevations and camera views so known openings can be represented accurately and unresolved decisions flagged.
