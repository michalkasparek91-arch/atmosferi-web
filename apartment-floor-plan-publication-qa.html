Apartment floor plan QA before your development website goes live

A good-looking floor plan can still show the wrong home. Use this release checklist to reconcile the drawing, unit reference, published PDF, interactive selector and sales information before buyers see them.

Treat a floor plan as publishable information, not just an image

An apartment plan may look polished while still being wrong for the buyer. The building selector shows one layout, the downloadable PDF another, and the agent's inventory record uses a third apartment number. These are not cosmetic problems. They make it difficult for a visitor to identify the home they are considering and for sales staff to answer reliably.

This guide deals with the release of unit-specific plans onto a property development website. It is not a guide to designing an interactive floor selector or maintaining availability statuses. Its job is narrower: define a repeatable review of each published plan, the data printed beside it, its revision and the people authorised to approve it. Start with a spreadsheet and a controlled release process before buying a complicated platform.

Separate design drawings from marketing-approved information

A construction drawing, an architectural concept plan, a furnished marketing plan and a contract attachment may represent different states of the same apartment. None should silently substitute for another. Establish which document is the approved source for each published attribute, and whether the web asset is a faithful derivative of that document.

Ask the project architect to verify the architectural geometry against the agreed design source; ask the sales or development owner to approve the public unit identity and description; and obtain any necessary legal or compliance review for market-specific claims. A drawing marked “approved” for one purpose is not automatically authorised for public marketing or sale. Record the reason for each release, not just the person's initials.

Use one stable apartment ID across every touchpoint

Building, floor and apartment labels can change as marketing evolves. A stable internal unit key should not. Use it to connect the master inventory, floorplate hotspot, unit detail page, PDF download and enquiry. Keep the visible marketing name as a separately editable field.

For example, a visitor might see “Residence 2B” while sales uses the internal key B-204. The PDF, filter results and enquiry should still resolve to the same underlying unit. Verify this relationship in both directions: select a hotspot and read its detail; open the detail by URL and confirm that the highlighted hotspot is the same home. Never identify a unit only from its position in an image or its place in a list.

Do not mix area definitions or silently round them

“Floor area” is not a universal field. Gross internal area, net usable area, balconies, terraces, storage rooms and shared spaces may be defined and disclosed differently across projects and jurisdictions. The public website must use the basis approved for that particular development, with a consistent label, units and rounding rule. Do not copy a number from a CAD measurement into sales copy without verifying what it measures.

If the brochure gives one figure and the website another, resolve the discrepancy at the authoritative source before publishing either. Store values and their basis as distinct fields where needed. A balcony should not unexpectedly appear inside an apartment's internal area total, and a unit type's illustrative size should not be presented as the exact size of every home of that type. Project advisers should approve any required qualifications.

Check the geometry a buyer is likely to misread

Review the apartment outline, entrance, room arrangement, window positions, outdoor spaces and orientation against the approved source. Look especially for mirrored apartments, rotated floorplates, reused furniture layouts and identical unit types with subtly different terraces. A copied “typical floor plan” is not enough where individual homes differ.

A drawing with no scale can still communicate room relationships, but it should not imply that furniture, door clearances or dimensions are guaranteed. If dimensions are shown, check their values, notation and basis. If a north arrow or view direction appears, ensure it describes the same orientation as the building selector. Never infer a guaranteed outlook from a marketing rendering when the underlying evidence does not support it.

Maintain a release register that the whole team can understand

Keep a small, shared register for every plan that goes live. It should tell a developer precisely which asset to publish, which unit it belongs to, what revision is authoritative and who approved that release. Export an accessible plan, not an unreadable thumbnail

A plan should remain usable on a narrow mobile screen. Offer a legible larger image or appropriately prepared PDF where detailed reading matters, and provide a text-based route to the same essential facts. Check contrast, labels, text size and orientation after export. Avoid making a tiny diagram the only route to understanding a unit.

If the plan is interactive, every selectable home needs a readable name and a keyboard-operable equivalent in the unit list. On a downloadable PDF, check whether its text remains selectable and meaningful rather than being a single opaque raster image. Sensitive technical information that has no marketing purpose should be removed through a deliberate approval process, not casually hidden behind a crop. The web version should retain accurate credits and lawful publication rights.

Make a revision replace every public copy of the plan

When a unit changes, the visible plan, downloadable file, floor-selector preview, comparison card, brochure link and sales reference must be reviewed together. Do not replace only the image on the detail page while leaving an older PDF accessible from a search result or shared link. A release checklist should enumerate each place the unit is represented.

Use a traceable revision and deployment record. Depending on the platform, new file names or cache invalidation may be needed so older copies do not continue to appear. Decide what happens to superseded public PDFs: provide a redirect to the current equivalent when appropriate, or retire them deliberately, while preserving internal records for audit. A timestamp in the CMS does not prove the CDN or an old bookmarked document has updated.

Keep apartment design facts separate from availability

Plan revision and sales status have different owners and different update rhythms. A design update might change the layout, while a sales update changes whether the home can be enquired about. Neither event should silently overwrite the other. A plan's “approved for publication” state does not imply that the apartment is for sale today.

Where status data is unavailable or stale, the website should avoid a confident availability claim and direct the visitor to confirm with sales. The separate apartment availability workflow explains how to assign source-of-truth ownership, handle failed feeds and avoid contradictory public status labels. This guide focuses instead on the integrity of the plan and its linked unit record.

Run buyer and editor acceptance tests before launch

Do not stop at a visual sign-off in a design tool. Test the published experience using a real staging URL, approved assets and a person who has not prepared the content. The following scenarios should pass on mobile and desktop, without a hidden dependence on hover or a particular browser.

Fictional example: the mirrored apartment nobody noticed

Fictional example, not a real Atmosferi client, case study or measured outcome. Imagine a development with two nearly identical apartments: B-203 and B-204. The design office updates B-204 to show a mirrored kitchen and terrace. The marketing team receives the new drawing, but the website still points both floorplate hotspots to the original image. A buyer opening B-204's detail sees an unchanged plan while the PDF has the revised layout.

The release register reveals the mismatch: the approved source for B-204 is Rev D, but the published image is Rev C. The team pauses publication of the updated plan, checks the unit mapping and revises the floorplate hotspot, image, PDF and captions as one release. A second reviewer opens the website from a new browser session to verify that the correct version appears. No conversion increase, sales result or real-world incident is asserted here.

Put ownership and change control into the website brief

At handover, nominate who authorises a drawing, who enters unit data, who publishes web assets and who checks the result. Ask for a demonstration of adding a new unit plan, replacing a revision, undoing an incorrect release and finding the source behind a public number. Record which documents the website team may edit and which require sign-off from the design or sales lead.
