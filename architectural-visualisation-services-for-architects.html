One visual language, different architectural formats.

Some commissions need one carefully composed image to communicate a house. Others need a coherent collection of viewpoints showing an entire residential development or an interior sequence. The right format follows the audience and design stage, rather than an arbitrary number of images.

Images for design decisions, competitions and project communication.

Design development: A rendering can help an architect or client understand how a proposed volume, finish or space will feel. Views should make unresolved decisions visible rather than quietly replacing them with invented answers.

Competitions and presentations: The composition needs to communicate a concept quickly and clearly. An expressive image can still preserve key geometric, contextual and material constraints. When view positions are prescribed, they take priority over a more dramatic camera.

Property development: Consistent imagery across exteriors, shared amenities and interiors helps visitors understand a proposal before construction. Views, planting, fit-out and surrounding buildings should follow approved information, with illustrative details identified when necessary.

Studio portfolios and press: A smaller set of carefully art-directed images may communicate a design better than many near-duplicates. We can plan crops for websites, presentations and publication from the outset, instead of discovering at delivery that every frame was composed only for a widescreen slide.

A transparent rendering workflow keeps the design in control.

  1. 01 / Scope. Define the audience, number of views, project stage, source documents, intended usage and decision-maker.
  2. 02 / Camera and geometry. Establish the most useful viewpoints and confirm building proportions, critical details and visible context before detailed image finishing.
  3. 03 / Materials and mood. Develop a consistent lighting direction, facade and interior surfaces, landscape treatment and level of activity based on the agreed design.
  4. 04 / Review. Collect consolidated notes on previews and distinguish architectural amendments from artistic adjustments. Record the items approved at each stage.
  5. 05 / Final images. Check the agreed output formats, resolution, cropping, credit requirements and publishing needs before handover.

Design changes can happen late, but they should not be invisible in the schedule. A revised facade or changed room layout may require rebuilding more than one image. The rendering feedback checklist explains how to give comments that keep both speed and accuracy.

Photorealism should not come at the expense of your architecture.

Good architectural imagery balances composition, believable light and material response with the actual design. Glazing reflections should not erase the interior; vegetation should not conceal an unresolved facade; a beautiful sunset should not imply a particular solar condition where accuracy is required.

When dimensions, materials or context are still provisional, we identify what is approved and what is illustrative. In design reviews, it is often useful to keep comparison views at a consistent camera angle so that a material or facade option is not favoured simply because it has more flattering lighting.

For geometry-heavy projects, the BIM/CAD handover guide sets out what a useful model package contains. If perspective, surrounding buildings or landscape are sensitive, the relevant drawings and references need to be part of the briefing rather than an afterthought.

What to send when requesting a quote.

You do not need an elaborate presentation before making contact. A concise description and a few reliable files are enough to understand what needs to be clarified. A finished 3D model can save time, but a well-coordinated drawing set may also be a useful starting point.

  • Project type, design stage and deadline
  • Plans, sections, elevations and site information, ideally labelled by revision
  • Available CAD, BIM or 3D model and its file format, if one exists
  • Material schedule or reference images, with unresolved decisions highlighted
  • Desired views and the purpose of each image
  • Reference mood, season, vegetation and interior styling where relevant
  • Output size, publication channels and any required alternative crops
  • One person authorised to gather and approve feedback

The architectural rendering brief template goes through the input package in more detail and contains a copyable example. It is meant to reduce ambiguity, not to make your architect spend an afternoon filling in a form for a two-image commission.

Architectural rendering prices depend on scope, not just image count.

Atmosferi's published pricing currently lists architectural still images from €400 per image, excluding VAT. This is a starting point, not a fixed offer for every project. Complex geometry, incomplete models, specialist context, many design options or a compressed delivery schedule may change the work required.

A useful quote specifies the number and type of views, what modelling is included, review assumptions and final delivery files. The same building can share preparation across several views, but an interior and a distant aerial view do not necessarily take equal effort. Animation, interactive scenes, additional crops and the release of editable source files require explicit agreement rather than assumptions.

For an accurate estimate, send the intended imagery and available documentation. A bounded project scope is more helpful than an attractive but meaningless price per square metre.

Example: selecting a focused set of images for a residential project.

Fictional illustrative example, not a claim about an Atmosferi client or project result. Imagine an architect presenting a six-home development with a shared courtyard. The project team initially asks for eight exterior images. A short briefing clarifies three distinct decisions: the relationship to the street, the courtyard experience and the main living space's connection to its garden.

Instead of producing several similar images, the initial scope might include one street approach, one courtyard viewpoint and one interior view. Each has a defined audience and a design responsibility. The team also notes that landscape species are provisional, while massing, windows and external circulation are fixed. That clarification keeps the imagery useful for decisions and makes the quote easier to compare with alternatives.

Frequently asked questions.

Can you work from drawings without an existing 3D model?

Often yes, but the amount of necessary modelling and verification depends on the completeness of the documentation. We would review plans, elevations, sections and key dimensions before confirming the scope.

Can we change the materials after the first preview?

Materials can be reviewed as part of the agreed process. Larger architectural changes after approval may require additional work, so the quote should clarify review stages and change scope.

Do you also make websites for architecture studios?

Yes. Atmosferi combines architectural imagery with digital presentation and website design for architecture practices. The services can be commissioned separately or considered together when a project needs both content and a publishing platform.

Are you available for international projects?

Collaboration can be organised remotely, with shared files, annotated previews and scheduled reviews. The practical questions are the project brief, the decision-maker and the required delivery dates.

Can the final images be used for print and web?

Yes, provided the image size, cropping and agreed publishing rights match the intended use. Print dimensions, digital formats and source-file requirements should be agreed before production.

Send an overview of your project, the available documentation and the visuals you have in mind. We can identify suitable viewpoints, a realistic deliverable list and what still needs to be resolved before an accurate quote.
