A floor plan selector is not just a graphic. It is a small piece of software connecting the architectural drawings, unit inventory and sales conversation. The goal is a confident buyer decision, not another animation for its own sake.

What can an interactive floor plan include?

Clickable building or floorplate

Identify a building, floor and apartment visually. Selecting a unit opens its reference, layout and approved information. The building view can remain a lightweight 2D diagram when that communicates the project most clearly.

Unit comparison and filters

Browse by room count, floor, area or verified sales status when those fields are available. A readable list or table keeps the same information accessible without using the interactive drawing.

Connected enquiries

Carry the selected apartment identifier into a project-specific enquiry. Depending on the agreed scope, this may connect to the developer's sales inbox, CRM or an existing availability system, with explicit data ownership and failure handling.

2D, clickable or 3D?

For a small development, clearly labelled plans and a unit list may be all that is needed. For a larger building with many similar apartments, a clickable elevation or floorplate can make location and orientation much easier to understand.

A 3D selector adds value only when it answers a genuine spatial question, such as how a unit relates to the tower, courtyard or view. It also adds modelling, loading and testing work. I recommend the simplest interaction that lets a buyer complete the task.

A clear scope before the first screen.

01 / Architecture & assets

Approved plans, buildings, unit keys, floor numbering, orientations and visual assets. Confirm which drawings are marketing material, whether all versions match, and what may be published.

02 / Buyer journey

Choose which decisions the visitor should make: discover a home, compare options, open a plan, shortlist units or send an enquiry. Prototype the phone layout and an equivalent keyboard-accessible text route before building richer graphics.

03 / Data & handover

Define who maintains apartment information, how statuses are approved, which systems exchange data and what the website should do if an update fails. Test the entire route from selection to received sales enquiry.

For development teams evaluating an interactive floor plan service provider, the useful comparison is not just how convincing a 3D demo looks. Ask what the selector will actually publish, how it stays accurate, and who owns it after launch.

How to choose an interactive floor plan service provider.

01 / Test the actual buyer journey

Ask each provider to show a functioning example of comparable complexity. Try selecting a unit, opening its plan and submitting an enquiry on a phone, with a keyboard and without the visual selector. Our Meridian demo is a fictional working concept, not evidence of a completed client commission.

02 / Ask how unit information stays correct

A provider should explain how unit identifiers map to plans, where approved areas and sales status originate, and what happens when an integration stops updating. Request separate ownership for drawing revisions and commercial availability. A polished interface cannot repair contradictory source records.

03 / Make handover part of the offer

Agree which files, content editing permissions, integrations, documentation and post-launch checks are included. The team that will operate the selector should be able to update a plan, verify an availability change and recover from an incorrect publication without depending on guesswork.

Do not require 3D by default. A well-designed clickable 2D floorplate with a reliable unit list may be the better deliverable. Ask providers to describe the simplest solution that passes the project's acceptance tests.

One apartment. One consistent identity.

Every view should use the same permanent unit identifier. The floorplate, unit card, PDF plan, availability record and enquiry form must refer to the same apartment. Otherwise, a visually impressive selector can send the sales team the wrong request.

A project may have separate sources for design information and commercial status. We agree who confirms unit geometry, plans, room counts, price visibility and reservation status. A website should not promise real-time availability if nobody can guarantee a reliable source of updates.

See the operational checklists for keeping apartment availability accurate and passing unit context into sales enquiries.

Built for real phones, not only presentation screens.

Small apartment polygons, hover-only labels and heavy 3D scenes can become unusable on a phone. A floor filter, readable unit list and clear plan view often work better for mobile buyers than forcing them to manipulate a miniature building.

The agreed scope includes responsive states, meaningful text labels, keyboard interaction where relevant, image-loading fallbacks and an accessible route to every unit. Availability must not rely on colour alone, and no critical enquiry action should be hidden behind a visual-only selector.

Search engines should also be able to understand the real page text and project information. An interactive drawing does not replace structured, crawlable content for the development and its units.

What to send for a quote.

Your development

A short description, approximate number of buildings and units, target buyers, planned launch date and any approved brand direction. A public project location is helpful, but not mandatory for an initial conversation.

Your source material

A sample floorplate, one approved apartment plan, sample unit data and any current sales website or CRM workflow. Early-stage files are enough to assess feasibility; sensitive documents can be handled through an agreed secure channel.

Your priorities

Tell me whether you mainly need an intuitive unit explorer, an architectural presentation, CRM handoff, content editing or several languages. I can then propose a suitable approach and scope rather than assuming that every project needs 3D.

For comparable proposals, ask each supplier to specify the number of buildings and units covered, whether plans and data are supplied or created, 2D versus 3D deliverables, available filters, source of truth, website or CRM integrations, mobile and keyboard testing, revision rounds, and who maintains the published selector. The unit-plan publication QA checklist can serve as a release acceptance reference. A price without these boundaries is not a comparable quote.

Frequently asked questions.

Can an existing 2D floor plan be made interactive?

Often, yes. We first check the drawing quality, unit geometry, identifiers and permitted publication format. A clean SVG or diagram may be sufficient. An entirely new 3D model is not always necessary.

Do I need a separate website to use a floor selector?

Not necessarily. The selector can be designed as part of a new development website or scoped as a component for an existing site. Integration depends on the current technology, design system and how unit data is managed.

Can apartment availability update automatically?

It can be considered if the development has a reliable, authorised source and a defined data contract. Automated updates are not assumed or promised without assessing that source, ownership, refresh frequency and failure behaviour.

How much does an interactive floor plan cost?

Pricing depends on unit count, the diagram or 3D assets, filtering, mobile and accessibility requirements, integration with existing data and testing. A simple clickable floorplate and a multi-building 3D selector are different scopes. A project brief is the right starting point for a specific proposal.

Can buyers still find apartments without the graphic?

That is the intended baseline. Every unit should remain discoverable through a readable list or other accessible path, including on mobile and when the interactive graphic is unavailable.
