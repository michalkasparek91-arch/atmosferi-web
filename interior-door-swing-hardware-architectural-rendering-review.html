How to review interior door swings and hardware in architectural renderings

A drawing-led method for checking door handing, hardware, movement and frame details before interior CGI is approved for publication.

Why door details deserve their own image review

A door that looks elegant in a still image can be impossible to operate in the space shown. A render may reverse the hinge side, omit a closer, hide a pull handle behind the leaf or depict a pocket door where the drawing specifies a hinged one. These are not simply styling differences: they change the architectural story a developer or practice communicates. This guide concentrates on door operation and hardware in interior CGI, rather than furniture selection, entry intercoms or lift equipment. It is a visual coordination process, not certification of accessibility, fire safety or door performance.

Start with the door schedule and the correct revision

Request the current dimensioned floor plan, door schedule, interior elevations, hardware set, wall types and any specialist door details. Note the drawing revision and distinguish approved items from provisional selections. Confirm whether the view depicts a design proposal, a marketing specification or an existing space. A generic asset-library door may have a different frame, thickness and hardware configuration from the specified assembly. Do not quietly substitute one because it photographs better.

Check handing from the plan, not from intuition

For each visible door, identify the hinge side and the direction the leaf opens on the coordinated plan. Handing conventions vary, so annotate the plan with an actual swing arrow rather than relying only on an ambiguous left-hand or right-hand label. Compare the model from both sides of the opening. A mirrored 3D asset or negative scale can reverse the handle and hinges while leaving the room apparently plausible. If the door is shown open, its position must correspond to the approved swing and available wall space.

Model the frame, leaf and reveal as a system

Check the structural opening, finished wall thickness, frame depth, architrave or flush reveal, undercut and threshold against the elevations and details. A frameless door is not interchangeable with a concealed-frame product. Verify whether the door is single, paired, sliding, pocket or pivoting and whether the chosen camera exposes a false connection at the head or jamb. Keep real floor finish transitions visible instead of painting them out in post-production.

Treat hardware as specified components

Check lever or pull type, rosette or backplate, latch, lock, thumbturn, hinge visibility, stop and any closer or hold-open device. Review both faces where the design differs. A door with a pull on the corridor side and a lever on the room side should not inherit the same generic handle asset on both sides. Do not invent a brand, certified rating or accessibility feature from the appearance of a 3D model. If a hardware set is unresolved, mark the image as provisional in the review pack.

Show the real swept area

Inspect the door in closed and partially open positions, ideally with a plan overlay. The swept area may conflict with cabinetry, seating, sanitary fixtures, glazing or another door. Do not move furniture or narrow the leaf solely to clear a composition without recording a design change. For sliding doors, check the actual travel path, pocket or surface track, pull clearance and the position of nearby switches. A single beauty shot cannot demonstrate an unobstructed route.

Escalate fire, acoustic and accessibility questions

A door may form part of a compartment boundary, acoustic separation or accessible route. These properties cannot be inferred from a photorealistic image. Confirm visible components and clearances with the responsible architect and specialist documentation. Where the design calls for seals, a closer, vision panel or signage, show what is documented. Do not label a rendered assembly as compliant or assign a fire rating because it resembles a catalogue product.

Use a coordination camera alongside the hero image

Keep the atmospheric marketing perspective but add a straight-on view and a plan crop for review. The frontal view reveals handle height relationships, frame proportions and alignment. The plan crop exposes handing and interference that the perspective hides. Record the camera and model revision. Avoid hiding a door leaf, widening an opening or changing the lens to make a conflict disappear. If a cutaway is necessary, label it as a review view rather than a literal built perspective.

Fictional example: a compact apartment hallway

Fictional illustrative example, not an Atmosferi commission or verified building. A marketing render shows a bathroom door opening outward into a hallway with a slim console table. The approved plan shows the door opening inward, and the hardware schedule calls for a different handle set. The visualisation team compares the plan and model, corrects the swing and handle, then issues a frontal review crop. The marketing image is revised only after the architect confirms the inputs. No claim about statutory clearance or operational certification follows from the image.

Record discrepancies instead of retouching them away

Use a small issue register with the door ID, drawing revision, observed discrepancy, owner, decision and affected camera. Separate model corrections from unapproved design changes. When a door schedule changes after an image has been signed off, flag every affected still, animation frame and virtual tour view. This is particularly important when a developer has already distributed images to sales teams or agents.

Pre-publication door review checklist

Before export, confirm the correct schedule revision; leaf type and handing; opening angle; frame and reveal; visible hinges and hardware; adjacent finishes and thresholds; conflicts with furniture and other doors; documented specialist components; and the status of unresolved selections. Have the architect sign off the design representation and the visualisation lead sign off image consistency as separate decisions.
