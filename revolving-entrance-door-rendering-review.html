How to review revolving entrance doors in architectural renderings

A practical visual QA guide to revolving entrance door geometry, wing positions, facade interfaces, adjacent access and truthful presentation images.

By Michal Kašpárek, architect and founder of Atmosferi22 November 2026

Define the visual decision

A revolving entrance may be the visual centre of a public building, but a compelling perspective does not show whether the entrance has been coordinated. Define the image's purpose first: architectural appearance, a comparison of entrance options or a record of the proposed configuration. Request the current plan, section, elevation, door schedule and available supplier information.

Record the drawing revision on every review output. Separate design questions from technical certification. A rendering cannot approve pedestrian capacity, accessibility, escape strategy or mechanical safety.

Use the selected system rather than a stock model

Generic revolving-door models often have the wrong drum diameter, number of wings, hub shape or glazing divisions. Match the actual design documentation, including the entrance orientation and the location of the central assembly. Avoid copying product dimensions from photographs.

If the supplier is not yet selected, label the representation provisional and identify which details are assumptions. A polished material pass should not turn a placeholder into an apparently approved product.

Choose a believable wing position

The rotation angle changes what can be seen through the drum. A wing may conceal the exit or create the false impression of a straight open passage. In plan, overlay the illustrated wing position on the proposed enclosure and check that the visible divisions agree.

A single frame is an illustrative state, not proof of operating clearance. For animations, agree the motion sequence with the design team and supplier rather than inventing speeds, controls or user behaviour.

Review the facade and canopy junction

The entrance drum may project beyond the facade or sit partly within a recess. Check the actual projection, head detail, frame depth, roof or canopy connection and adjacent glazing grid. A frontal camera can hide an incorrect projection.

Use an oblique crop or section to reveal the enclosure. Keep visible weathering, structural and finishing interfaces faithful to the drawings; do not erase them for a cleaner marketing image.

Inspect the floor and threshold

External paving, internal floor finish and the drum threshold should follow the coordinated level strategy. A continuous floor texture can accidentally conceal a step, recess or entrance mat. Compare a low-level crop with the architect's section.

For related checks, consult the entrance mat recess guide and the floor finish transition guide. Neither a crop nor a render certifies the built threshold.

Show the adjacent alternative entrance

Where the approved design includes a separate swing or sliding door, show its relationship to the revolving entrance. Cropping it out of every presentation image can distort how the building is approached and used.

Represent its documented width, location and opening type, but leave accessibility and fire-safety compliance to the responsible specialists. The automatic sliding entrance guide addresses a different mechanism.

Coordinate visible equipment and glazing

Sensors, access-control devices, signage, lighting and emergency controls may be visible in the final design. Include specified elements and flag unknown locations rather than inventing hardware. Check glazing divisions and any required markings.

Curved glass can appear black or invisible depending on reflections. Use balanced reflections so the wings and enclosure remain readable; see the glazing reflection review.

Build a three-view approval set

Use an approach perspective for the architectural impression, a plan crop for wing orientation and an oblique or section view for enclosure depth. Keep camera identifiers, drawing revisions and provisional notes consistent.

Approve geometry under neutral lighting before preparing a dramatic dusk image. Keep a clean version without people so reviewers can see the full entrance, then add entourage only after the layout has been checked.

Fictional example: a library lobby

Fictional illustrative example, not an Atmosferi commission or built case study. A hypothetical library entrance is rendered using a generic four-wing asset. The architect's provisional plan shows a different arrangement and an adjacent accessible entrance omitted from the camera frame.

The artist replaces the asset with provisional documented geometry, widens the view to show the separate entrance and marks unresolved supplier details. No claim about capacity, certification, safety or accessibility is made.

Final review and approval boundary

Check the current supplier status, drum footprint, wing count and angle, facade projection, canopy connection, floor levels, adjacent door, specified equipment, glazing markings, entourage and consistency between views. List unresolved assumptions before publication.

Approval means the image represents the supplied design information at the stated revision. It does not certify revolving-door mechanics, fire performance, safe operation, accessible circulation or crowd movement.

Make the entrance geometry reviewable

Share the entrance plan, section, supplier status and camera brief so assumptions can be resolved before final visualisation.
