How to review shower linear drains and floor falls in interior renderings

Review shower linear drains, floor falls, tile setting-out and thresholds in architectural interior renderings without inventing construction details.

Why shower drainage is a rendering coordination issue

A walk-in shower can look perfectly flush in a hero image while concealing the very floor falls and drain position that make the design intelligible. This review is for architectural CGI teams checking whether a proposed linear drain, tile layout and threshold are represented consistently. A render cannot demonstrate waterproofing, hydraulic capacity or code compliance. Those remain the responsibility of the qualified design and installation team.

Start with coordinated drawings, not a product asset

Request the current bathroom plan, enlarged shower floor plan, sections, setting-out drawing, finish schedule, plumbing layout and selected drain details. Record revisions and identify which dimensions are approved and which are provisional. A library drain model with a convincing metal grate is not evidence of the specified product. If the plans disagree, ask the architect rather than silently relocating the outlet for a cleaner image.

Model the direction of falls deliberately

Check where the high and low points are intended and whether the design uses a single-plane fall, multiple planes or a formed shower tray. A large-format tile material projected across an entirely flat surface can erase an important change in plane. Avoid prescribing a universal gradient: the actual fall and tolerances depend on the system, jurisdiction and project details. Use section information to construct the surface rather than inventing a slope from appearance.

Locate the linear drain against the enclosure

A drain along the back wall, at the entrance or offset to one side produces different visible relationships with the shower screen, wall finish and threshold. Compare the drain centerline and length with the dimensioned drawing. Check its end clearances and whether a wall-adjacent installation is actually specified. Do not extend a generic grate edge-to-edge merely because the resulting line looks more elegant.

Keep tile joints and cuts consistent with the fall

Tile direction, grout width and cut pieces should follow the setting-out plan. A floor using multiple slopes may require diagonal cuts or a mosaic, whereas a single-plane fall can support a different layout. Neither should be invented to hide an unresolved design. Compare joints at the drain, wall corners and shower entrance, and make sure the same pattern survives every camera angle and close-up.

Review the wet-area threshold separately

A visually level transition is not automatically a technically flush, accessible or waterproof junction. Check the shower entrance detail, screen position, adjoining floor level and any specified profile. Where the detail is unresolved, keep the visualisation provisional and avoid language that implies a barrier-free installation has been certified. The broader floor finish transition review covers adjacent rooms and door junctions.

Coordinate glass screens with the drainage line

The shower screen may sit beside, above or away from the drainage channel depending on the approved design. Verify the glass fixing line, door swing if present and visible support hardware. A frameless screen rendered as a perfectly invisible plane can obscure the relationship between water containment and the shower entrance. Show its edges and reflections credibly without implying an unverified fixing method.

Check the drain against plumbing and fittings

Shower heads, niches, mixers and benches affect how the composition is read, but their positions do not establish water performance. Confirm the selected drain configuration and visible trim against the schedule. Never invent hidden pipe routes or trap clearances from a still image. If a fixture or niche conflicts with a section, record the design query and keep the CGI model aligned to the latest coordinated revision.

Render wet finishes without overstating performance

Wet tiles may have darker patches and highlights, but a glossy wet-look shader cannot prove slip resistance, drainage or water tightness. Confirm finish references, grout colour and drain trim before adding reflections. Avoid fake puddles as a shortcut to communicate slope. Where the visualisation is intended for marketing, distinguish atmospheric styling from specified surface characteristics.

Use a plan crop, section and eye-level view

The eye-level hero shot explains the bathroom atmosphere but often hides a low-profile drain. Pair it with a top-down or orthographic crop showing drain position and tile joints, plus a section-derived check of the fall and threshold. Keep the drawing revision and camera ID attached to each view. The point is to expose discrepancies early, not to turn a promotional render into an installation drawing.

Fictional example: a compact walk-in shower

Fictional illustrative example, not an Atmosferi client project. A fictional apartment bathroom shows a wall-length linear drain in the render. The fictional floor plan instead specifies a shorter channel offset from the glass screen, and the section indicates a single fall toward the rear wall. The visualisation artist flags the mismatch, replaces the generic channel with a neutral provisional model and requests the architect’s decision before exporting the final views.

Separate model errors from unresolved design decisions

Log the affected camera, drawing revision, exact mismatch, decision owner and status. A drain missing from an otherwise approved drawing is a model correction; incompatible plan and section positions are a design query. Mark each issue as open, resolved or awaiting confirmation. When a decision changes, update all stills, animations and property-website crops rather than leaving contradictory imagery in circulation.

A practical pre-publication check

Before publication, verify the drain location and length, visible trim, floor planes, tile joint setting-out, shower-screen line, threshold condition and finish schedule against the approved source. Confirm that provisional products are labelled and the fictional examples are not presented as completed work. Keep the technical limitations explicit: CGI sign-off is visual coordination, not proof of safe installation or compliance. For broader approvals, consult the rendering feedback checklist.
