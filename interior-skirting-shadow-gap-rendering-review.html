How to review skirting boards and shadow gaps in interior renderings

Check skirting profiles, recessed shadow gaps, wall finishes and corners against coordinated interior details before approving architectural CGI.

Why the wall-to-floor junction deserves a review

A carefully lit interior can make the base of a wall almost disappear. Yet a conventional skirting, flush baseboard or recessed shadow gap changes the apparent proportions of a room. An image that omits the specified detail can imply a cleaner construction system than the architect has designed. This guide is a visual-coordination checklist, not a construction specification, fire-performance assessment or proof of buildability.

Gather the documents that define the junction

Start with current interior elevations, wall sections, finish schedules, door details and any manufacturer drawings for the skirting or recessed trim. Identify which rooms use each system, including service areas and secondary corridors. Record drawing references and revisions before assigning a material to the render. A mood board showing a minimalist floating wall does not establish the geometry of a recessed base detail.

Distinguish applied, flush and recessed systems

An applied skirting projects from the wall face; a flush skirting aligns with the finished wall plane; a recessed shadow gap creates a deliberate visible separation. These are not interchangeable styling presets. Compare the specified section to the model at a corner and doorway. A dark texture strip painted along the wall base is not equivalent to a modelled recess with an actual edge and reveal.

Check heights against the finished floor

Verify the top of the skirting or recess relative to the finished floor level, not the structural slab. Changes in flooring build-up can shift the apparent base detail between rooms. Avoid using a single arbitrary height throughout a project if the issued drawings distinguish zones. Where the drawings show no dimension or a conflicting finish build-up, flag the missing information rather than inferring a precise construction dimension from a reference photograph.

Review internal and external corners

In a hero view, long straight walls can conceal poor corner connections. Inspect an internal corner, an exposed external corner and any short wall return. Check that profiles meet without unexplained overlaps, gaps or sudden changes in section. A recessed channel should follow the approved geometry around corners where specified, while an applied skirting may require a distinct end or junction detail. Do not invent mitres or proprietary fittings when the detail is undecided.

Coordinate door frames and concealed openings

Door openings reveal whether the base detail was actually coordinated. Compare the skirting or shadow gap with architraves, flush door frames, jamb returns and pocket-door openings. The base may stop, turn or continue according to the approved detail. A generic continuous black line passing through a doorway can be as misleading as a skirting that collides with the frame. Review door operation separately in the door swing and hardware guide.

Keep finishes and reflected light believable

Painted MDF, timber, metal and plaster-edge profiles have different reflectance and edge definition. Match the finish schedule instead of selecting a darker material solely to make the gap read in a small image. In grazing light, a recess should create a plausible change in light and shadow rather than a perfectly uniform black stripe. A neutral clay pass can help distinguish missing geometry from a lighting artifact.

Check cabinets, built-ins and wall panels

Kitchen plinths, wardrobes, fitted benches and timber wall panels often interrupt the base detail. Review their junctions in plan and elevation. A baseboard should not pass visibly through a built-in cabinet, and a recessed channel should not continue behind exposed furniture unless that is the documented arrangement. Keep the question of cabinetry toe-kicks separate from the architectural skirting specification.

Coordinate the floor edge without duplicating another review

Flooring material changes can alter the visual relationship at the wall base. Compare tile, timber or carpet build-ups with the relevant section, especially where a recess exposes an edge. For the separate issue of boundaries between two floor finishes and doorway thresholds, use the floor finish transitions checklist. This article focuses on the vertical wall-to-floor junction and its continuity.

Use one hero camera and two diagnostic crops

Review the intended marketing camera alongside a low oblique crop of a long wall and a close crop of a door or external corner. Use the same model revision for all three. At small scale, the line may be subtle; the diagnostic crops should still reveal its geometry. Do not hide an unresolved termination behind a sofa or plant. Record the camera identifier, drawing revision and decision owner with each discrepancy.

Fictional example: a small apartment living room

Fictional illustrative example, not an Atmosferi commission or an approved construction detail. An early interior model shows a continuous dark recessed channel below every wall. The revised interior elevation specifies applied painted skirting in the living room and a different junction at a flush hallway door. The visualisation team updates the room-specific profiles, checks two corners and submits annotated crops to the architect. The final image follows the approved drawings without claiming that the depicted assembly has been technically certified.

Separate rendering corrections from unresolved design

Use a short issue register: room, junction, source drawing, model revision, visible discrepancy, responsible person and status. A missing skirting shown clearly in an issued elevation is a modelling correction. A contradictory section and finish schedule is a design query. Do not resolve the latter by choosing the most photogenic option. Recheck stills and animation shots after changing the base geometry, since shadows and reflections may shift across several outputs.

Pre-release wall-base checklist

Confirm the specified system in each room; verify finished-floor reference; inspect internal and external corners; check door jambs and architraves; coordinate cabinetry and wall panels; compare finish and reflectance; review at least two diagnostic crops; document unresolved details; and preserve the approved source revision. The aim is faithful communication of the design, not an invented technical sign-off.

Keep interior junctions faithful to the drawings

Share the current interior elevations and intended camera views to distinguish confirmed details from open design questions.
