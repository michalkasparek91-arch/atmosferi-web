How to review window reveal depth after facade insulation changes

A drawing-led workflow for checking window recesses when exterior wall insulation and finish thickness change.

Why a few centimetres can change the whole elevation

An exterior rendering can preserve the correct window dimensions and still misrepresent the facade. If an insulation build-up becomes thicker while the window frame stays in its earlier model position, the visible reveal deepens. That affects the shadow around the opening, the apparent size of glazing and the reading of the facade from the street. Conversely, moving a frame outward just to recover a shallow-looking reveal may suggest an installation position that has not been designed. This is a guide to checking visual fidelity against current drawings, not to specifying thermal performance, installation methods or weatherproofing.

Collect the drawings that actually control the recess

Request the current wall-type schedule, facade section through a typical window, window schedule, sill and head details, external finish build-up and elevation setting-out. Identify the structural opening, the outer finished facade plane and the intended frame position as separate references. Record drawing numbers and revisions. A wall thickness in a BIM model may omit render, cavity, battens or insulation layers; a generic library window may have its frame anchored to the wrong face. Do not infer a completed detail from a photorealistic texture.

Use three reference planes instead of one visual guess

Check three planes in the 3D scene: the structural wall or opening face, the finished exterior surface and the window frame's documented reference line. Compare their offsets in a perpendicular section, not only in perspective. Once these planes agree with the approved drawing, the reveal depth becomes a consequence of the model rather than an artistic adjustment. If a detail is unresolved, mark the offset as provisional in the review register. Avoid presenting a millimetre-precise CGI dimension as a measured construction value.

Separate insulation thickness from finish thickness

An external thermal insulation composite system, a ventilated rainscreen and a masonry cavity wall can all produce deep openings for different reasons. In a rendered facade, a continuous plaster texture may hide the underlying layers. Ask which finish face defines the final opening, whether a return has a different build-up and where the frame sits relative to that finish. Do not invent insulation continuity, cavity barriers or fixing details. The architect and envelope specialists must resolve those technical decisions.

Review sill projection, drip edges and lower returns

When the facade plane moves outward, an old sill model may no longer project beyond the finished surface, or it may appear to end inside the new reveal. Compare sill projection, slope, end returns and visible junctions with the issued detail. The visualisation should represent a documented geometry, not imply that water will drain correctly merely because a shadow line looks plausible. If the sill type has not been selected, identify it as an illustrative placeholder rather than modelling an unapproved proprietary profile.

Check heads, lintels and external shading together

A deeper reveal can shade the upper portion of the glazing and alter the apparent head height. It can also change the relationship to blinds, external louvers, roller-shutter boxes and canopy edges. Verify these elements against sections and elevations. Do not pull a blind box forward to make it visible in the hero camera unless that position is supported by the coordinated design. For window movement and hinge clearances, see the separate operable-window review; reveal depth is a wall-envelope geometry question, not an opening-mechanism question.

Look for inconsistency across repeated openings

Copying one window asset across a building can conceal different wall build-ups at the ground floor, upper floors, recessed balconies and extension interfaces. Review representative openings for each facade type, including corner windows and windows near material transitions. A repeated shadow band is useful for spotting a systematic offset error. Do not assume every opening should have an identical recess if the wall assemblies differ.

Test a frontal elevation and an oblique close-up

A frontal camera is useful for checking opening dimensions and alignment but weak at revealing recess depth. An oblique view shows jamb and head thickness, sill projection and the way the finished facade turns into the opening. Keep a neutral lighting version alongside the atmospheric hero image: strong low sun can make a correct reveal look excessively deep, while flat exposure can hide a modelling error. Compare at the actual intended publication crop and resolution.

Fictional example: an apartment facade revision

Fictional illustrative example, not an Atmosferi commission or an approved construction detail. A residential scheme changes from a thinner external wall finish to a thicker insulation and render build-up. The first CGI update moves the exterior wall surface but leaves the window objects and sills untouched. The oblique view now shows deep jambs and sills that disappear into the wall. The architect issues a revised window section and confirms the frame reference line and sill type. The artist updates those elements, exports the same two cameras and logs which openings were affected. The image does not certify thermal bridges, fixings or moisture protection.

Keep the design decision and image correction separate

Use an issue record with facade zone, window ID, current drawing revision, expected exterior plane, frame reference, sill status, affected camera IDs and the person who owns the unresolved decision. Mark issues as either “render differs from issued design” or “design detail not yet confirmed.” The first is a visual correction; the second requires a design response. Preserve both the before and after crops so later facade changes can be checked without relying on memory.

Pre-publication review checklist

Before release, confirm the exterior finish plane, frame position and wall build-up against current sections; check jamb, head and sill geometry; verify that repeated windows reflect the right wall type; review shading devices and material transitions; compare frontal and oblique cameras; identify provisional details; and keep an approval record. For other facade-line questions, use the facade movement-joint guide. For sash operation, consult the operable-window guide. To brief a coordinated exterior visualisation, contact Atmosferi.
